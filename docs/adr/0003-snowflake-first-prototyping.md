# 0003 — Prototype on Snowflake first

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-02
- **Deciders:** Playbook maintainers (Stephen Morleys)
- **Unit of Work:** none; predates Jira tracking for this repo

## Context

Prototypes under AI-DLC exist to answer a question in days, on real data, cheaply enough to throw away ([Chapter 05](../../playbook/05-rapid-prototyping.md)). The slowest and most compliance-laden step of any prototype is moving governed data somewhere new. Our governed data lives in Snowflake, with RBAC and masking policies already applied.

Snowflake now offers an application surface inside its perimeter: Streamlit in Snowflake for UI, Snowpark for logic, Cortex for LLM features, Streams and Tasks for event-like pipelines, Container Services for anything else. An agent can generate a usable internal app from a schema and a question in one Bolt.

## Decision

We will prototype on Snowflake by default: Streamlit UI, Snowpark logic, Cortex for AI features, generated by the primary agent from the Unit of Work and deployed the same day. Alternatives are chosen by the Chapter 05 heuristic: where is the data, who touches it, does the question need production-shaped infrastructure.

Prototypes keep the security and governance controls of real data. Promotion is a rebuild against production standards, never a rename.

## Options considered

### Option A — Snowflake-first  ← chosen

- Pros: no data movement; existing governance applies automatically; nothing to host; one-Bolt time to a demo.
- Cons: Streamlit in Snowflake trails open-source Streamlit; not a customer-facing surface; per-viewer compute needs a warehouse sanity check.

### Option B — Supabase + Next.js first

- Pros: fastest path for product-shaped, customer-facing prototypes with auth and realtime.
- Cons: governed data must be extracted or synthesized; a second security perimeter to manage.
- Rejected as default because: most of our prototype questions are internal and data-shaped. Kept as the named alternative for customer-facing UX.

### Option C — AWS serverless first

- Pros: highest fidelity to the production architecture; exercises IAM and event flows.
- Cons: slower first mile; most questions do not need production shape to be answered.
- Rejected as default because: it answers "will this shape work" better than "is this worth building". Kept as the typical second prototype.

## Consequences

- Positive: prototypes run on real data by default, which kills fake-data theater; cost per answered question is measurable in warehouse credits plus tokens.
- Negative: platform limits will be hit; the playbook treats hitting them as a promotion signal, which requires discipline.
- Follow-ups: `snowflake-prototype` skill ([skill template](../../standards/skill-template.md)); monthly prototype sweep by review-by date.

## Revisit when

Governed data moves to a different gravity well (a lakehouse, a different warehouse), or Streamlit in Snowflake stops tracking open-source Streamlit closely enough that pods fight the platform routinely, or a majority of prototype questions in a quarter are customer-facing.
