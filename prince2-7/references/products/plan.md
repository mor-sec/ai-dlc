# Plans: project, stage, team, exception

**Purpose:** says what products will be delivered, how, by whom, when and at what cost. It is the baseline the work is measured against.

| Plan | Created | Approved by | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project plan | IP; updated at each SB | Project board | High level; shows management stages |
| Stage plan | SU (initiation stage), then SB for each subsequent stage | Project board | Detailed; covers one stage |
| Team plan | MP (optional) | Team manager, agreed with the PM | Supports a work package |
| Exception plan | SB, when the board requests it after an exception report | Board (stage level) or business layer (project level) | Replaces the plan it relates to, from the current point to its end |

## Composition
1. **Plan description**: what the plan covers and the planning approach.
2. **Prerequisites**: what must be in place for the plan to succeed.
3. **External dependencies**.
4. **Planning assumptions**.
5. **Lessons incorporated**.
6. **Monitoring and control**: how progress will be tracked and reported.
7. **Budgets**: time and cost, plus risk budget and change budget where used.
8. **Tolerances**: for all seven performance targets as they apply at this level.
9. **Product descriptions**: for the products in scope (at project level, the project product description at least).
10. **Schedule**: product breakdown structure, product flow diagram, activities, dependencies, resources, milestones. Use a Gantt chart or a timeboxed backlog for agile work.

An **exception plan** additionally restates the exception report's recommended option and shows the new forecast against the original tolerance.

## Planning technique (product-based)
1. Write the project product description.
2. Build the product breakdown structure, covering both management products and specialist products.
3. Write a product description for each significant product.
4. Draw the product flow diagram (sequence and dependencies).
5. Identify activities and dependencies, estimate, schedule, analyse risk and document.

## Quality criteria
- Every product in the schedule has a product description or is clearly trivial.
- The schedule is achievable: resources are assigned and not overloaded, and dependencies are realistic.
- Tolerances and budgets are stated and consistent with the level above.
- Assumptions and external dependencies have owners and matching risks in the register.
- The plan is at the right level of detail for its horizon.

**AI notes:**
- Estimate agent-executed work in elapsed time *and* cost (tokens or compute), and include human review effort. Review is often the bottleneck.
- Plan bolts or sprints inside stages, not as stages.
