# Project brief

**Purpose:** gives the project board enough to decide whether to initiate the project. It is not a baseline.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Created in** | Starting up a project (SU) |
| **Prepared by** | Project manager |
| **Approved by** | Project board, in DP (authorise initiation) |
| **Derived from** | Project mandate, discussions with the executive and senior users, lessons from similar projects |

## Composition

1. **Project definition**:
   - background
   - objectives across the seven performance targets
   - desired outcomes
   - scope and exclusions
   - constraints and assumptions
   - project tolerances (set by the business layer)
   - users and other interested parties
   - interfaces
2. **Outline business case**: reasons, options considered, expected benefits, and rough cost and time. See `business-case.md`.
3. **Project product description**: see `product-description.md`.
4. **Project approach**: delivery approach (linear, iterative or hybrid), make, buy or reuse, and the operational environment the products must fit.
5. **Project management team structure and role descriptions**: names for executive, senior user(s), senior supplier(s) and PM, plus any known assurance arrangements.
6. **References**: the mandate and relevant lessons.

## Quality criteria

- It is brief: a few pages. Detail belongs in the PID.
- Objectives are measurable, and tolerances are stated or explicitly TBC with an owner.
- Scope says what is out, not only what is in.
- The project approach has been chosen and reasons are given.
- The board covers all three interests: business, user and supplier.
- Relevant lessons are referenced.

**Ends with:** decision required. Authorise initiation, with the initiation stage plan attached or referenced.
