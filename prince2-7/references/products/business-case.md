# Business case

**Purpose:** documents the justification, so the board can judge whether the project is (and remains) **desirable, viable and achievable**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Created** | Outline in SU (inside the project brief); detailed in IP (in the PID) |
| **Updated** | At every stage boundary (SB), on exception, and at closure (CP) |
| **Owner** | Executive (the PM usually drafts it) |
| **Approved by** | Project board; the business layer may also need to approve it |
| **Derived from** | Project mandate or brief, project plan (time and cost), risk register, benefits management approach |

## Composition

1. **Executive summary**: the case in a few lines and the recommendation.
2. **Reasons**: why the project is needed and how it fits strategy.
3. **Business options**: at minimum *do nothing*, *do the minimum* and *do something*. Give the reasons for the chosen option.
4. **Expected benefits**: measurable, each with a baseline, target, measurement method, timing and owner (senior user). Classify them as cash or non-cash.
5. **Expected dis-benefits**: negative outcomes someone will perceive.
6. **Sustainability impact**: positive and negative, consistent with the sustainability management approach.
7. **Timescale**: project duration and benefits realisation period.
8. **Costs**: project costs plus ongoing operations and maintenance costs.
9. **Investment appraisal**: net benefit over time, and NPV/ROI/payback if the organisation uses them. Include a sensitivity analysis.
10. **Major risks**: summary of the aggregate risk profile and key threats and opportunities.

## Quality criteria

- Each benefit is measurable, has an owner, and can be traced to a project outcome.
- Costs match the project plan, and the risk summary matches the risk register.
- Options include "do nothing", and the chosen option is justified.
- Assumptions are visible and the sensitivity analysis covers the biggest ones.
- At each update it says plainly whether the project is still **desirable, viable and achievable**.

**Stage boundary update:** add a short "changes since last version" section and a clear statement: *justification remains / is weakened / no longer holds*, with the reason.

**AI notes:** include model and compute costs for both build and run. Treat productivity gains from AI agents as assumptions, back them with evidence from early stages, and revisit them at each boundary.
