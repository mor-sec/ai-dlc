# Tailoring PRINCE2 7

What you can tailor: processes (combine or lighten), management products (merge, split, change format), roles (combine, but never the executive with the PM if the board must hold the PM to account), terminology, and reporting frequency and medium.
What you cannot tailor: the seven principles.

The PM records tailoring decisions in the PID (a "tailoring" section) and the project board approves them. Revisit them at each stage boundary.

## Tailoring drivers

Scale, complexity, risk, importance, team capability and maturity, delivery approach, regulatory context, and whether the project sits inside a programme or a supplier contract.

## Patterns

### Small or low-risk project

- One person may be executive and senior user. The PM may also act as team manager, with no separate project support.
- Merge the project brief and PID into one document, and merge SU and IP when the mandate is clear.
- Use two stages minimum: initiation plus one delivery stage.
- Highlight reports can be a short message or email at an agreed cadence. Checkpoint reports can be a stand-up note.
- Keep the project log as one spreadsheet or folder of CSVs. Risk and issue registers can share one sheet if each row has a `type` column.
- Management approaches can be a single page with one paragraph each, saying "use organisational standard X" where one exists.

### Agile / iterative-incremental delivery

- Management stages contain several timeboxes (sprints). Stage boundaries align with release or funding decisions, not every sprint.
- Tolerances: fix time and cost tightly and flex scope (MoSCoW on features). Quality tolerance stays tight.
- Work package = a sprint commitment or epic, with the backlog as the product breakdown. Product descriptions ≈ epics with acceptance criteria and a definition of done.
- Checkpoint report = sprint review / burn-up. Highlight report = a roll-up of sprints for the board.
- Product owner ≈ user-facing representative feeding the senior user. Scrum master ≈ team manager support. Keep the board's role intact.

### Inside a programme

- The programme is the business layer. It sets project tolerances and may provide the approaches (risk, benefits, quality, data, sustainability).
- Reference the programme standards instead of rewriting them, and state where the project deviates.
- The benefits management approach is usually owned by the programme, and the project contributes outputs and outcomes.

### Supplier-side project

- The customer's executive owns the overall business case. The supplier keeps its own business case for its margin and risk.
- The commercial management approach is central: contract type, change control across the contract, payments against milestones, and acceptance.
- Senior supplier on the customer board may be the supplier's account lead.

### Regulated or safety-critical

- Strengthen quality: independent assurance, formal reviews, and traceability from requirement to product to test.
- Hold more stages and keep tolerances tighter.
- Make the digital and data management approach and the issue management approach explicit about audit trail.

## How to recommend tailoring

Produce a table with columns *Element | Standard | Tailored as | Reason | Approved by/date*. Keep it to the elements you are changing. End with the board decision needed.
