# PRINCE2 7 method core

## Five integrated elements

1. **Principles**: seven obligations that make a project a PRINCE2 project.
2. **People**: leadership, stakeholders, culture, teams and change management. New in v7.
3. **Practices**: seven aspects managed continuously; called "themes" in v6.
4. **Processes**: seven step-wise processes from pre-project to closure.
5. **Project context**: the environment the method is tailored to.

## Seven principles (cannot be tailored away)

| Principle | What it means in practice | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Ensure continued business justification | The project stays desirable, viable and achievable; it is re-checked at every stage boundary | Business case updated each stage; board can and will close early |
| Learn from experience | Lessons are sought at start-up, recorded throughout and passed on at closure | Lessons log used, lessons report issued |
| Define roles, responsibilities and relationships | Business, user and supplier interests are all represented; relationships, not just roles | Named board members, role descriptions, stakeholder engagement |
| Manage by stages | Plan, monitor and control one management stage at a time; minimum two stages (initiation plus at least one delivery stage) | Stage plans; board authorises each stage |
| Manage by exception | Tolerances are delegated per level, and only forecast breaches are escalated | Tolerances set per level; exception reports used |
| Focus on products | Agree what is to be produced (and its quality criteria) before doing the work | Product descriptions before work packages |
| Tailor to suit the project | Adapt processes, products, roles and terms to the context | Tailoring recorded in the PID and approved by the board |

## Seven practices

| Practice | Question it answers | Key products |
|---|---|---|
| Business case | Is it still worth doing? | Business case, benefits management approach |
| Organizing | Who is accountable for what? | PID (roles section), communication and change management approaches |
| Plans | What, how, by whom, when, how much? | Project, stage, team and exception plans; product descriptions |
| Quality | Will the products be fit for purpose? | Quality management approach, product descriptions, quality register |
| Risk | What uncertain events could affect objectives? | Risk management approach, risk register |
| Issues | Something has happened. What do we do, and how do we control change? | Issue management approach, issue register, issue report |
| Progress | Are we on track, and should we escalate? | Tolerances, checkpoint, highlight, exception and end stage reports |

People-related concerns (adoption, culture, stakeholders) sit in the **people element**, not in a practice.

## Seven processes

| Code | Process | Owner | Trigger → main output |
|---|---|---|---|
| SU | Starting up a project | Executive / PM | Project mandate → project brief, initiation stage plan |
| DP | Directing a project | Project board | Runs throughout: authorises initiation, the project, each stage, exception plans, closure; gives ad hoc direction |
| IP | Initiating a project | PM | Authority to initiate → PID (incl. full business case, project plan, approaches) |
| CS | Controlling a stage | PM | Stage authorisation → work packages, highlight reports, issue and risk handling, corrective action |
| MP | Managing product delivery | Team manager | Work package → products, checkpoint reports, quality register updates |
| SB | Managing a stage boundary | PM | Stage end approaching or exception → next stage plan or exception plan, updated business case, end stage report |
| CP | Closing a project | PM | Final stage end or premature close → acceptance, handover, end project report, lessons report |

## Roles and the three interests

- **Business layer** (corporate, programme or customer): commissions the project, appoints the executive and sets project tolerances.
- **Project board**:
  - **Executive** (business interest; owns the business case and is ultimately accountable).
  - **Senior user(s)** (user interest; specifies needs and owns benefits realisation).
  - **Senior supplier(s)** (supplier interest; provides resources and technical integrity).
- **Project assurance**: independent of the PM and accountable to the board, covering business, user and supplier assurance.
- **Change authority**: decides requests for change within a delegated change budget. The board may delegate this.
- **Project manager**: runs the project day to day within stage tolerance. One person.
- **Team manager**: delivers work packages within work package tolerance. The role is optional; the PM may cover it.
- **Project support**: administration, tools and records. It serves the PM, is separate from assurance, and is optional.

## Tolerances and the seven performance targets

Targets: **benefits, cost, time, quality, scope, sustainability, risk.**
Each can carry plus and minus tolerance at three levels:

| Level | Set by | Held by | If forecast to breach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project | Business layer | Project board | Board escalates to the business layer |
| Stage | Project board | Project manager | PM raises an **exception report** to the board |
| Work package | Project manager | Team manager | TM raises an issue to the PM |

Typical expression:

- cost and time: ± amount or %.
- scope: must-have products fixed; could-haves can be dropped (MoSCoW).
- quality: ranges in quality specifications.
- benefits: ± on target value.
- risk: maximum exposure, or no single risk above a threshold score.
- sustainability: limits on carbon, waste or social measures.

**Exception test:** is a breach of an agreed tolerance *forecast*? If yes, escalate up one level now, without waiting for the breach. An issue that can be absorbed within tolerance is handled at the current level.

## Who decides?

| Situation | Decider |
|---|---|
| Deviation within work package tolerance | Team manager |
| Deviation within stage tolerance | Project manager (corrective action) |
| Stage tolerance forecast breached | Project board (via exception report; may ask for an exception plan) |
| Project tolerance forecast breached | Business layer |
| Request for change | Change authority (or the board) |
| Business case no longer viable | Project board (may close the project early) |
| Tailoring | Recommended by the PM, approved by the board |

## Issue types

- **Request for change**: a proposal to change a baseline.
- **Off-specification**: something that should have been or will be provided but isn't, or doesn't meet its specification.
- **Problem / concern**: any other matter needing the PM's attention.
- **Business opportunity**: an unexpected positive possibility.

Issue technique: capture → assess → recommend → decide → implement.

## Risk

- Describe each risk as cause → event → effect.
- Technique: identify → assess → plan → implement, with communication throughout.
- Threat responses: avoid, reduce, transfer, share, accept, prepare contingent plans.
- Opportunity responses: exploit, enhance, share, accept, prepare contingent plans.
- **Risk appetite** is an attitude set by the business. **Risk tolerance** is a measurable threshold that triggers escalation.
- Risk owner manages the risk; risk action owner carries out the response. Risk budget funds responses.

## Quality

- **Quality planning**: user quality expectations → acceptance criteria → product descriptions with quality specifications.
- **Quality control** (inside the project): reviews, tests and inspections, recorded in the quality register.
- **Quality assurance**: independent of the project team. It checks the process, not the product.

## Plans

- Levels: project plan, stage plans, optional team plans, and exception plans (which replace the plan they relate to).
- Product-based planning: write the project product description → build a product breakdown structure → write product descriptions → draw a product flow diagram → then estimate, schedule and analyse risk.
- Delivery approach: linear-sequential, iterative-incremental or hybrid. PRINCE2 governs any of them; management stages are not the same as delivery iterations.
